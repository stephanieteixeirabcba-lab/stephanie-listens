what I teach

Practical, and built to use tomorrow.

Every course ends with something you made — a script, an agenda, a rewritten goal, a probe you can run this week. If you finish a session of mine without an artifact in your hands, I've done it wrong.

Supervision that isn't a status update

Structuring the meeting, giving feedback that changes the next session, and running competency probes that measure skill rather than attendance.

Supervising what you can't see

Remote competency assessment, coaching through a screen, and catching clinical drift when there are no hallway conversations to catch it in. Five years of telehealth supervision, distilled.

Catching a struggling supervisee early

The indicators that show up before a crisis does, the check-in question that actually surfaces a problem, and what to do with the answer.

Goals a family can actually read

Rewriting objectives out of jargon and into language a caregiver recognises as their own child. Done live, on real goals.

Building goals with families, not for them

Culturally and contextually responsive goal-setting when a family's values, language, or priorities differ from the ones the assessment assumed.

When a client declines

Assent as behavior rather than philosophy — what you actually do in the next thirty seconds, and how to document it.
